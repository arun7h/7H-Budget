Executive summary

This report maps the 7H FY2027 budget and business plan cycle, from issue of the template in September 2026 to budgets loaded into BC, PW and EPS in December 2026. It draws on the Month 6 Week 3 deck on version control, the “Business Plan - MASTER Jan..Dec Financial Year” template (Implementation Timeline tab), and the Group Finance Lead’s email of 9 Sep 2026, “Budget Timelines Jan 2027 Budgets”.

Ardoe HouseBirmingham DeltaDalhousie CastleIslay HouseHoliday Inn LeicesterThornbury Castle

The six hotels named in the email. Other 7H properties may follow the same process.

The workflow

18 steps across five lanes, with three decision points: Senior Team review of Draft 1, Senior Team review of Draft 2, and the CEO/Owner call, where each version is agreed or amended.

The versions

Each hotel budget passes through five versions: business plan, Draft 1, Draft 2, Owner pack and Final. Only the first template version is evidenced so far. Everything from 14 Oct onwards is Planned.

The issues

13 version-control issues: 8 in the template, 2 in the email and 3 known problems in practice. The template has no version log, no protected cells and one step with no named owner.

The fix

One naming convention and a ten-point protocol: one master per hotel, no desktop copies, locked formulas, shaded input cells, a Version Log tab and a version freeze at each review gate.

Confidentiality. People are anonymised by role: Group Finance Lead, Senior Team Members A–E, Revenue Managers A–D, CFC 1–n, Functional Leads A–B and CEO. Hotels are named.

How it works in practice

On the shared drive

The master budget file for each hotel is kept on the shared drive. Each named person fills in their own tabs, and their names are marked on the tabs they own. Teams catch-ups agree who does what, and fortnightly progress calls track deadlines.

CFCs and Revenue Managers each cover 3–4 hotels. That is why steps 3, 6, 9, 10 and 14 run in parallel across hotels, and why wrong-hotel entries happen.

My decision point

As Director of Operations on the Senior Team, I review each hotel’s draft (steps 7 and 11) before it goes to the CEO. The CEO then holds a review call with the Senior Team and the Owners (step 13) to agree or amend that version. The CFC makes any amendments and resubmits them as the next version.

Rework loops: Draft 1 → amend → Draft 2 (steps 7–10) · Draft 2 → amend → v2.x (step 11) · Owner pack → amend → Final (steps 13–15).

The three risks (from the deck)

Work overwritten

Two people save changes to the same file, and whoever saves last wins.

Trail breaks

A change goes in with no note of what, why or who.

No one owns the mistake

A wrong edit cannot be traced to a person, so it cannot be fixed at source.

P7

Recommended file-naming convention

7H_BP27_[HotelCode]_[Stage]_v[Major].[Minor]_[YYYY-MM-DD].xlsx
HotelCode
A short fixed code per hotel, e.g. ARD, BHD, DHC, ISL, HIL, THC
Stage
TPL · BP · D1 · D2 · OWN · FINAL
Major.Minor
Major goes up at each gate (1 = Draft 1, 2 = Draft 2, 3 = Final). Minor goes up for each logged change inside a stage, e.g. v2.1 after Senior Team amendments.
Date
Date saved, in ISO format so files sort in order
7H_BP27_THC_D1_v1.0_2026-10-30.xlsx7H_BP27_THC_D2_v2.1_2026-11-27.xlsx7H_BP27_THC_FINAL_v3.0_2026-12-14.xlsx

Never use “MASTER”, “FINAL2”, “latest” or initials in a file name. Who made the change goes in the Version Log, not the file name.

Recommended version-control protocol

  1. P1
    Name every file to the convention

    Finance sets the name when it issues the file. Only the version and date parts change after that.

    Fixes issue 1
  2. P2
    One master per hotel, in one controlled folder

    Finance creates the six masters and nobody else may create one. Share a link to the file, never an attachment. Superseded versions move to a read-only Archive folder and are never deleted.

    Fixes issues 10, 11
  3. P3
    No downloads or desktop copies

    Edit only in the shared copy. Finance data stays off local devices (B8).

    Fixes issue 12
  4. P4
    Add a Version Log tab as the first tab

    Columns: Version · Date · Who (role) · Tab/cells · What changed · Why · Approved by. Every save that changes figures gets one line, including template amendments and review-call actions. Do not take change requests by email.

    Fixes issues 2, 7, 9
  5. P5
    Mark the owner on every tab

    Put an owner banner (role and name) in row 1, and use one tab colour per owner role (Revenue, CFC, GM/HM, Finance).

    Fixes issue 6
  6. P6
    Lock formula cells and shade input cells

    Protect every sheet. Unlock and colour-shade only the cells people should fill in, and add a colour key on the Implementation Timeline tab.

    Fixes issue 3
  7. P7
    Lock the hotel identity

    Hotel name and code are entered once, on Hotel Summary, from a drop-down. Every other tab shows them by formula in the header. Before submission, the reviewer checks that the header matches the file name.

    Fixes issues 5, 13
  8. P8
    Name an owner for every step

    No step owner is left as “???”. Finance owns the protocol and the folder.

    Fixes issue 4
  9. P9
    Freeze the version at each gate

    When D1, D2 or the Owner pack is submitted, it becomes read-only. Reviewers comment. The CFC then saves the next minor or major version and logs each change. No edits are made to a version while it is under review.

    Fixes issue 8
  10. P10
    Brief people before kick-off

    Run a short session on this protocol for Revenue, CFCs and hotel teams before the template is issued. Put the full timeline in their diaries at the same time.

    Supports all of the above
04
Collaboration reflection

What I learned working across teams

B8S17
What I did

During the budget cycle I worked with the Revenue teams, the CFCs and the hotel senior teams, including Sales Managers, C&B Sales Managers and General Managers.

We used Teams catch-ups to agree who would do what, and fortnightly progress calls to track each deadline in the timeline.

What went wrong

Three problems affected the integrity of the budget files. Duplicate master files were created by accident, so work was split across two copies. Some CFCs downloaded the file and worked on it on their desktops, outside the shared master. Revenue Managers and CFCs sometimes entered one hotel’s data in another hotel’s tab.

Looking at these through the Month 6 material, each one is a version-control failure rather than a people failure. They risk work being overwritten, a broken audit trail and mistakes that nobody can trace back to their source.

What I’d do differently
  • Start the process earlier, so everyone knows the deadlines well in advance.
  • Give clear guidance on who fills in which tabs.
  • Train stakeholders on version control and managing changes before budgeting starts.
  • Stop anyone downloading the file and working on their desktop.
  • Lock formula cells so they cannot be overwritten.
  • Colour-shade the cells that need input, so it is clear where people should enter data.

These changes would make the budget files more secure and easier to trace. They are the professional working standard that B8 asks for, and the version protocol in Section 3 puts them into practice.
